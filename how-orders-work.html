From their side

What happens when a customer orders

  1. They fill the bag

    From a product page or the shop grid, Add to Bag. The bag survives closing the browser.

  2. They open Checkout

    Four things are collected: full name, phone number, address, and wilaya. Payment is fixed — Cash on Delivery. There is no card payment on the site at all.

  3. They tap Place Order

    The order is written to your database immediately with the status pending. Their bag empties, and the stock of each product is reduced by the quantity ordered.

  4. They get an order number

    A confirmation screen shows DL- followed by the first eight characters of the order's ID — for example DL-C1CA0D1F. That number is how you and the customer will talk about the order.

Your side

Where the order lands

Open /admin and choose Orders in the left rail. You never have to go looking for an order — the page goes looking for them.

  • Refreshes itselfEvery 15 seconds. Leave the page open and new orders appear on their own.
  • New-order alertA green "N new orders received!" message, a chime, and — if you allow it once — a notification from your browser. The Orders tab also keeps a pulsing "new" badge until you open it.
  • The four tilesTotal orders, Pending, Shipped, and Revenue. Revenue ignores cancelled orders.
  • Find one fastThe search box matches a customer's name, their phone number, the order number, or their address. The status chips across the top filter the list, and the number on each chip is a live count.
The alerts only work while the page is open. There is no server behind this shop sending you an SMS or an email — the browser page itself is what watches for new orders. Keep /admin open on a phone or on the shop computer during opening hours, and click Allow the first time it asks about notifications. If you close the tab, nothing will tell you an order arrived; you will simply see it the next time you open the page.

Reading an order

Everything one row expands into

Click any order to open it. You get the order number and the exact time it arrived; the customer's name, phone, address and wilaya — the phone number is a link, so on a phone you tap it to dial; every item with its photo, quantity and line price; and the total to collect.

The five statuses

Moving an order along

Every order starts at Pending and moves only when you move it. Nothing advances by itself. Open the order and tap the status you want — that is the whole system, and it is how you know at a glance which parcels still need you.

Status 1

Pending

The order has just arrived and nobody has touched it. This is your to-do list.

Status 2

Processing

You have seen it, checked stock, and it is being packed.

Status 3

Shipped

It has left you — handed to the courier or the delivery company.

Status 4

Delivered

The customer has the parcel and has paid the courier. This is a finished, paid order.

Status 5

Cancelled

It is not happening — refused, unreachable, returned. Cancelled orders are left out of Revenue.

Changing a status is a note to yourself. It does not message the customer — nothing is sent automatically. The one place a customer can see it is My Orders on their own account, if they have one.

The routine

A day at the order desk

Four habits in the right order. The second one is the one that saves money.

  • Morning — filter to Pending That list is the day's packing list. Nothing else on the page needs you yet.
  • Call before you ship Ring every new customer, confirm the address and the amount to collect. This single call is the cheapest way to avoid a parcel that comes back to you.
  • Mark Processing, then Shipped Processing when it is confirmed and packed, Shipped the moment it leaves with the courier.
  • Evening — close the loop Filter Delivered and mark what arrived. Filter Cancelled and see what fell through. Treat the Revenue tile as turnover, not as cash in your hand — money is only real once a delivery is paid.

Their view

What the customer can see

  • If they signed in/my-orders lists the order with the status you set and the total.
  • If they ordered as a guestThey still get the confirmation screen with their order number, but the order will not appear under My Orders on their account. Ask them to keep the number — it is their only record.
  • What they never seeYour other customers' orders, your internal notes, or anything at all inside /admin.

The money

What the customer pays

  • CurrencyAlgerian dinar throughout — 168,750 DZD style formatting.
  • Payment methodCash on Delivery only. You take no money on the site; the customer pays whoever hands them the parcel.
  • Added at checkoutThe site adds 8% to the subtotal, shown as tax. Shipping is free once the subtotal passes 75 DZD — in dinar that threshold is so low it is always met, so shipping is effectively always free.
  • So the amount to collectExactly the total on the order card. Nothing else is owed on delivery.
  • Worth a decisionThe 8% tax and the 75 DZD free-shipping threshold look like leftovers from a dollar-based template. In Algeria, delivery is a real cost of a few hundred dinar. Have a look at whether you meant to charge for it — it is a one-line change in src/stores/cartStore.js.
